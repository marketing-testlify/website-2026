What is attrition rate?

The attrition rate is the percentage of employees who leave your organization compared to the total number of employees during a specific period. This metric helps you understand the workplace culture, employee job satisfaction, and whether your compensation is fair.

Attrition rate calculator formula

Attrition rate = (Number of employees who left \u00f7 Average number of employees) \u00d7 100

Number of employees who left: the total number of employees who left during the period.

Average number of employees: the average headcount during the period, calculated as:

Average number of employees = (Employees at start + Employees at end) \u00f7 2

How to calculate the attrition rate

Imagine your company had 150 employees at the start, 140 employees at the end, and 18 employees left during the quarter.

Step 1 \u2014 average number of employees:
(150 + 140) \u00f7 2 = 290 \u00f7 2 = 145.

Step 2 \u2014 attrition rate:
(18 \u00f7 145) \u00d7 100 \u2248 12.41%.

18 employees left during the quarter, out of an average headcount of 145 \u2014 an attrition rate of 12.41%, meaning about 12.4% of employees left during the quarter.

How does the attrition rate calculator help your business?

Tracking your company's attrition rate provides valuable insights into various aspects of your business, helping you make informed decisions and improve your organization.

  • Understand employee retention: a high attrition rate \u2014 usually over 20% \u2014 could indicate job satisfaction, work culture, or compensation issues that must be addressed to retain talent.
  • Identify problems early: regularly calculating your attrition rate helps you spot trends early \u2014 a spike may signal poor management, lack of growth opportunities, or low morale.
  • Assess workplace culture: employee turnover often directly reflects workplace culture, prompting you to evaluate leadership and engagement efforts.
  • Measure HR strategy effectiveness: if recruitment, development and retention programs are working well, attrition should be lower \u2014 if not, it's time to adjust your approach.
  • Improving hiring practices: high turnover may mean employees aren't a good fit \u2014 analyzing attrition helps refine recruitment and onboarding.
  • Cost management: high attrition is costly due to hiring, training and onboarding expenses \u2014 tracking it helps you take proactive steps to reduce turnover.
